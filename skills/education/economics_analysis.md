# 💰 Economic Thinking Framework

## Core Economic Principles

### 1. Scarcity and Choice
- Resources are limited, wants are unlimited
- Every choice has a trade-off
- Opportunity cost = value of next best alternative

**Socratic Questions:**
*"What is being given up to pursue this choice?"*
*"How would you allocate scarce resources?"*

### 2. Supply and Demand
- Price signals allocate resources
- Shifts vs. movement along curves
- Elasticity measures responsiveness

**Socratic Questions:**
*"What would happen to price if supply increases?"*
*"Why might demand be inelastic?"*

### 3. Markets and Efficiency
- Competitive markets are efficient
- Market failures require intervention
- Externalities and public goods

**Socratic Questions:**
*"Is this market efficient? Why or why not?"*
*"What are the externalities here?"*

### 4. Economic Growth
- Determinants of growth
- Productivity improvements
- Investment and innovation

**Socratic Questions:**
*"What policies promote growth?"*
*"Why do countries grow at different rates?"*

### 5. Macroeconomic Objectives
- Economic growth
- Low unemployment
- Price stability
- Balance of payments

**Socratic Questions:**
*"What are the trade-offs between these objectives?"*
*"Which objective is most important right now?"*

## Microeconomics vs. Macroeconomics

### Microeconomics
**Focus:** Individual markets, firms, consumers

**Key Topics:**
- Consumer theory
- Production theory
- Market structures (perfect competition, monopoly, oligopoly)
- Factor markets

**Socratic Questions:**
*"How does this firm maximize profit?"*
*"What determines market power?"*

### Macroeconomics
**Focus:** Economy as a whole, aggregates

**Key Topics:**
- GDP measurement and growth
- Inflation
- Unemployment
- Fiscal and monetary policy

**Socratic Questions:**
*"What are the causes of inflation?"*
*"How does monetary policy affect unemployment?"*

## Economic Models and Diagrams

### Common Models
1. **PPF (Production Possibilities Frontier)** - Trade-offs
2. **Circular Flow Model** - Money and goods flow
3. **Supply and Demand Graph** - Price determination
4. **AD/AS Model** - Macroeconomic equilibrium
5. **IS-LM Model** - Interest rates and output

**Learning Strategy:**
- Draw the diagram first
- Label axes and curves
- Identify equilibrium
- Understand shifts and changes

### Graph Interpretation
**Questions to Ask:**
1. What do the axes represent?
2. What is the relationship?
3. What causes shifts?
4. What does equilibrium show?
5. What are the implications?

## Economic Thinking Skills

### 1. Ceteris Paribus
- "All other things being equal"
- Isolate cause and effect
- Consider what changes and what stays constant

### 2. Marginal Analysis
- "How much more?"
- Compare marginal benefit vs. marginal cost
- Optimize decisions at the margin

### 3. Incentives
- "What do they have to gain or lose?"
- People respond to incentives
- Unintended consequences

### 4. Opportunity Cost
- "What is being sacrificed?"
- Every decision has a trade-off
- Think about all alternatives

### 5. Positive vs. Normative
- "What IS" vs. "What SHOULD be"
- Positive = objective, testable
- Normative = subjective, value-based

## Common Economic Fallacies

| Fallacy | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fallacy of Composition** | What's true for one is true for all |
| **Post Hoc Fallacy** | Correlation equals causation |
| **Broken Window Fallacy** | Destruction creates economic gain |
| **Zero-Sum Fallacy** | One person's gain is another's loss |
| **Sunk Cost Fallacy** | Continuing because you've invested |

## Economic Data Analysis

### Key Economic Indicators
1. **GDP** - Gross Domestic Product
2. **CPI** - Consumer Price Index (inflation)
3. **Unemployment Rate**
4. **Interest Rates**
5. **Stock Market Indices**
6. **Housing Starts**

### Interpreting Data
**Questions to Ask:**
- What is the trend?
- What might be causing this?
- How does this compare to historical data?
- What are the implications?

## Exam Strategies for Economics

### Essay Questions
1. Define terms clearly
2. Use economic diagrams
3. Explain logic step by step
4. Apply to real-world examples
5. Evaluate alternative perspectives

### Multiple Choice
1. Eliminate obviously wrong answers
2. Watch for qualifying words (always, never, only)
3. Think like an economist (marginal analysis)
4. Consider ceteris paribus

### Calculations
1. Write down the formula first
2. Show all work
3. Check units
4. Interpret the result

## Guided Economic Analysis

**Student asks:** "Why is inflation happening?"

**Tutor Response:** 
```text
Let's analyze this systematically:

1️⃣ What do we mean by inflation?
[Define: sustained increase in price level]

2️⃣ What are the possible causes?

    Demand-pull: too much money chasing too few goods

    Cost-push: rising production costs

    Built-in: expectations of inflation

3️⃣ What data would you need to identify the cause?

    Money supply growth

    Wage growth

    Production costs

    Consumer spending

4️⃣ For our current situation, which cause seems most likely?
[Guide them to analyze and reach a conclusion]

💡 Remember: Economists disagree! What matters is the reasoning.
```

## Economic Mnemonics

**Supply and Demand Shifters:**
- T=Taxes and Subsidies
- R=Related Goods Prices
- I=Income
- B=Buyer Expectations
- E=Number of Sellers
- S=Seasonal Factors

**Key Economic Schools:**
- Classical = Market self-corrects
- Keynesian = Government intervention needed
- Monetarist = Money supply matters
- Austrian = Individual choices matter
